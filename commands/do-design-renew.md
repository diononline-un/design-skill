# do-design-renew

Replace an existing look on purpose while keeping product truth: repositioning, a stale
brand, a surface that never worked. The incumbent is evidence about what does not work,
never a foundation.

1. **What survives**, written down before anything else:
   product truth (what it does, features, pricing logic) · content (meaning of the copy,
   real numbers, real testimonials) · function (flows, states, required fields) · native
   affordances (nav position, cart, checkout, login) · constraints (legal text, a11y,
   embedded third-party widgets).
   `Survives: … / Replaced: the visual world (palette, type, imagery, composition, motion)`
2. **Audit the incumbent** with the `do-audit` measurements and score. Then name what it
   got right: you are replacing a look, not a product, and something usually works.
   It survives as a pattern, not as a component.
3. **Research in full**: `layers/06-research.md`. Without it a renewal lands on the next
   tasteful default and needs renewing again in two years. Decide explicitly whether
   this is "fit the category properly" or "depart decisively"; clients rarely say which.
4. **Replacement world, then stop**: the five direction lines plus
   ```
   Keep from the incumbent:  <the thing that worked>
   Kill from the incumbent:  <the thing that defined the old world>
   Risk:                     <what the audience might misread>
   ```
   Wait for a yes: a renewal is approved visually before a line is written, so it is the
   most expensive thing to get wrong.
5. **Replace, don't polish.** The failure is the half-measure: violet softened to slate
   on the same layout is the template in a new colour. The old look gets no credit unless
   it is on the survival list. One dominant move the old world could never make. Never
   split the difference ("half the old palette for recognition" means undecided).
6. **New paper**: write DESIGN.md v2.0 as a new document with a new §1, not a diff;
   archive v1.0 in `docs/archive/`.
7. **Build** in the seven passes of `do-design-new`. Build the hero twice, conservative
   and fully committed, and show both: the first is what the client says they want, the
   second what they hired you for.
8. **Migrate** whole surfaces in order of traffic. Ship the top one complete, not all of
   them half-done; name it as the ✅ precedent in v2.0 §10.
9. **Report**
```
Mode: Persuade
Survives: <list>   Replaced: <list>
Direction: <five lines + keep/kill/risk>
Paper: v1.0 -> v2.0 (v1.0 archived)
Score before -> after: 47 -> 78
Migrated: <surface>; remaining: <list in order>
```

Failures: new colours on the old composition; a hero that claims what the product does
not do; skipping research because "we know what we want"; showing one option; no
migration plan.
